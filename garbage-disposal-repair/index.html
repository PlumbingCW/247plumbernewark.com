Disposal jammed or leaking right now: what to do first

Start with the switch. Turn the disposal off, then unplug it or switch off its breaker before touching anything underneath. A spinning blade can injure you even when the unit seems dead.

  • Switch the wall control off, then unplug the unit or turn off its breaker.
  • Look for the red reset button on the bottom of the motor housing and press it once if it popped out.
  • Find the hex socket in the center of the underside and work a hex wrench back and forth to free a jam.
  • Never reach into the grinding chamber. Use tongs or pliers to lift out a spoon, bone or bottle cap.
  • Restore power and run cold water while you test it.

If the reset pops again immediately, the motor is overheating or the winding is failing. That is the point where repair costs start to approach replacement, and a plumber can tell you which makes more sense.

A leak at the top where the disposal meets the sink flange is often a dried-out putty or gasket seal. A leak from the side or bottom points to a rusted housing, and that unit cannot be repaired. Leaks at the dishwasher hose or drain elbow are cheaper to fix than a full replacement.

Dishwasher connection

Most disposals have a small knockout port that the dishwasher drain hose attaches to. If a new disposal was installed without removing the plug, the dishwasher cannot drain, and dirty water backs up into the dishwasher. If the dishwasher fills with gray water after every cycle, check that the plug was knocked out and that the hose runs up in a high loop before it connects.

A blocked disposal can also push water back toward the dishwasher. The cure is clearing the line and the trap, not replacing either appliance. When the whole kitchen drain keeps slowing down, the cause may be in the pipe beyond the disposal, which is where drain cleaning takes over.

Repair or replace

Repair makes sense for a simple jam, a reset problem or a leaking seal on a unit that is a few years old. Replace when the housing leaks, the motor burns out or the unit is noisy and weak after years of use.

Replacement includes a new mounting assembly, a fresh drain connection and the electrical connection. Choose horsepower for how you actually cook. A lower-power unit is fine for light use, and a larger one handles heavier food waste and runs quieter.

Wiring, permits and who can do the work

A disposal is usually hardwired or plugged into an outlet under the sink. If your kitchen has no switched outlet under the sink, one has to be added, and new wiring is a different category of work. Newark's code excludes the installation of new wiring from the minor-repair exemption, which means a new circuit or receptacle needs a permit and a licensed electrician.

Delaware lets a homeowner do plumbing other than gas piping in their own non-rental home if a permit application is filed (24 Del. C. § 1807(c)(2)). The code does not say whether a like-for-like disposal swap needs a plumbing permit. Ask Newark's Code Enforcement office if your parcel number starts with 18, or New Castle County Land Use for any other parcel.

You can verify a plumber's license at delpros.delaware.gov. If the job also means moving the drain or adding fixtures, that falls under plumbing installation.

Keeping fats and grease out

The City of Newark's sewer guide advises keeping fats, oils and grease out of drains, along with rags and paper products, because they cause blockages and sewage backups. EPA's septic guidance also says never to pour cooking oil or grease down the drain, and the same habit protects a sewer lateral. Grease cools in the pipe and builds up into a plug.

Under Newark's code, a stoppage in the lateral from grease or soap leaving the house is the owner's responsibility to clear. Scrape plates into the trash, wipe pans before washing, and run cold water for several seconds after grinding. Do not grind stringy vegetables, bones or coffee grounds in large amounts, and never use a disposal as a trash can.

In unincorporated areas such as Brookside, New Castle County sewer rules apply instead, and they bar shredded garbage larger than half an inch from the public sewer.
