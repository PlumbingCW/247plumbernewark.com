What to tell the dispatcher

Start with the street address, the town and the ZIP code. Then say what is happening, what you have shut off and whether water is near anything electrical. If the property is a rental, a townhome or an apartment, say so, because shared walls, shared drains and landlord rules change who has to act and who can give access.

If the call is about a sewer backup, tell us which fixtures are affected. A single slow sink and a tub that gurgles when the washer drains point to different parts of the system, and the answer shapes what the plumber brings.

Homes, rentals and small businesses

Service is the same whether you own a house, rent a townhome or run a small shop. For rentals, tell the dispatcher who can give access and whether neighbors share the wall, floor or drain line. For a business, ask about after-hours work, since many commercial repairs are easier when the building is empty.

Why rules change from town to town

Two houses on the same street can fall under different offices, so never assume a neighbor's experience applies to you. Delaware's state plumbing regulation follows the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), while the City of Newark's code adopts the 2018 edition. Each office decides how it applies to your job.

Plumbing work is permitted and inspected by whoever has authority over the parcel. In the City of Newark, that is the City's code enforcement office. For unincorporated areas of New Castle County, it is the county land use office. State Division of Public Health plumbing permits may also apply. Across the state line, Elkton follows Maryland rules. We point out which office applies on each town page, and the plumber explains it again on site.
