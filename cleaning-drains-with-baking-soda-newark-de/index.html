Cleaning Drains With Baking Soda in Newark, DE: What It Can and Cannot Do

Baking soda and vinegar are fine for a kitchen sink that smells, but they are a weak tool against grease, hair and wipes.

Written by Newark 24/7 Plumbing · 2026

Baking soda and vinegar can loosen a thin film and freshen a smelly drain in a Newark, DE kitchen or bathroom, but they rarely clear a real clog. A stopper of hair, a grease plug or a wad of wipes sits too far down and is too dense for a few minutes of fizzing. If water is still standing after one attempt, stop and move to a better tool or call for help.

That is not a knock on the method. Used for what it is good at, it is cheap, safe for most pipes and gentler than store-bought openers. The mistakes come from expecting it to do a plumber's job.

This guide covers what the fizz really does, the steps that work better, and the point where a stubborn blockage calls for drain cleaning in Newark, DE rather than another round of home remedies.

What baking soda and vinegar actually do

Baking soda is a mild base and vinegar is a mild acid. Mixed, they bubble and form mostly salty water, so the reaction uses itself up fast. The fizz can break loose a light coat of soap film near the top of a drain, which is why the sink smells better afterward.

No official agency tests or endorses the trick, so treat it as a kitchen habit, not a proven fix. It does not dissolve hair, it does not cut through a hardened grease plug further down the line and it does nothing for a toothpaste-and-hair mass in a bathroom trap.

A sensible way to try it

If you want to give the fizz a go, do it once, do it correctly and judge the result right away.

  1. Clear what you can by handLift out the stopper or strainer and remove visible hair and gunk. This often fixes more than the fizz does.
  2. Add about half a cup of baking sodaPush it down the drain with a spoon or your fingers.
  3. Pour in a similar amount of white vinegarPlug the drain with a stopper or damp cloth so the bubbling stays in the pipe.
  4. Wait 10 to 15 minutesDo not run water while it works.
  5. Flush with hot tap waterLet the water run for a minute, then check the flow. If it is no better, switch tools.

Do not follow this by pouring a commercial drain opener on top. Mixing chemicals in a drain can release fumes, and any leftover liquid sitting in the pipe can splash back at whoever uses a plunger or snake next.

Skip the chemical openers

According to the U.S. EPA, households should skip chemical openers when a drain clogs. Its suggested alternatives are boiling water or a drain snake. EPA also advises against pouring solvents, oil-based paints or large amounts of toxic cleaners down the drain. That advice appears in its septic-system guidance, so read it as a general household caution rather than a Newark rule.

One practical note on hot water. Boiling water is fine for a metal or porcelain kitchen drain, but it can soften plastic pipes and loosen joints, so many plumbers pour very hot tap water into plastic lines. Never pour boiling water into a toilet, because porcelain can crack.

When a clog survives plunging, a hand snake and a hot flush, a plumber's hydro jetting service uses high-pressure water to scour the pipe wall, which removes grease build-up that a plain snake can punch through and leave behind.

What goes down Newark drains

Local note, Newark and New Castle County

Homes inside Newark city limits connect to a public sanitary sewer, which flows by gravity into the New Castle County system. The City's Sewer Care Guide lists what causes blockages and backups: fats, oils, grease, rags, diapers, sanitary napkins and any paper other than toilet paper. For you, that means scraping pans into the trash before washing them and keeping wipes out of the toilet.

The responsibility side matters too. Under Newark's sewer code, the owner clears stoppages in the lateral, the pipe from the house to the main, when they are caused by material such as grease or soap discharged from the house. A grease habit in your kitchen can turn into a plumber's invoice on your side of the property line.

The City's guide does not name wipes specifically, but EPA advises flushing only toilet paper and putting disinfecting wipes and other non-flushable items in the trash.

If the clog sits in the house drain, in fixtures that share a line or past the cleanout, the blockage is not a baking soda problem. That is the territory for sewer line cleaning, and for the City if the main under the street is involved.

Signs you are past the home-remedy stage

A single slow sink is a local problem. Several symptoms at once suggest a deeper one.

  • Water backs up in a floor drain or tub when you flush a toilet or run the washer.
  • Two or more fixtures drain slowly or gurgle at the same time.
  • The same drain clogs again within a week or two of clearing it.
  • A sewage smell lingers in the basement even after you flush traps with water.
  • You have already tried one round of plunging and one round of fizz with no change in standing water.

For a basement backup, ring the City's Water and Wastewater department first and ask for a check of the main, and then a plumber if the main is clear. We also take these calls in nearby communities such as Glasgow and Bear, where the sewer arrangement can differ from the City's, so confirm who maintains your main before you call anyone.

What the fizz cannot do for old pipes

Old drains build a coat inside the pipe. Grease sets, mineral scale grows and roots can enter at joints. Baking soda does not remove any of those, and repeated attempts can delay a diagnosis that a camera would settle quickly. If the same line clogs every few months, the cheapest long-term answer is usually a proper cleaning and a look inside, not another box of baking soda.
