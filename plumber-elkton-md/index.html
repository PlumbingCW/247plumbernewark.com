Maryland is not Delaware: licensing before the first wrench turns

Our dispatch is Delaware-based, and a Delaware plumbing license does not by itself authorize work in Maryland. Maryland licenses plumbers through the State Board of Plumbing, and Cecil County adds its own county master plumber license on top. Before any work starts in Elkton, ask the plumber who comes out which Maryland and Cecil County licenses cover your job, and confirm that on the call, not afterward.

You can check a Maryland license yourself on the state labor department's plumbing board page. Maryland also requires insurance for license holders, so ask about that too. If the licensing answer is unclear, say so before you agree to anything.

Whose pipe is it? Town side and owner side

Under Elkton's Town Code, the Town replaces a worn water or sewer lateral from the main to the property line at its own expense. Past that line, the owner pays, and a leak on the owner's service line means the owner pays for excavation and repair. Find your property-line cleanout now, since the code makes the owner responsible for installing one on new and replacement sewer laterals.

When sewage is backing up, a plumber can look into that cleanout and see whether the line is flowing. Water standing in it usually points toward a blockage downstream, which may be the Town's section or the main, while a dry cleanout points back toward the house. That one look decides whether you phone the Town's Public Works crew, which has an after-hours line listed on the Town website, or pay for a sewer line cleaning yourself.

If the line is old or has settled, a sewer camera inspection shows where it is cracked or offset before anyone digs. Elkton's code also calls for a permit for building sewer work, so expect the plumber to ask about it. The Town and Cecil County both point to the IPC, 2021 edition, so ask which office reviews your job under it.

Water: Big Elk Creek, wells and Inframark

The Town of Elkton runs its own water system, not a private water company. Its 2025 water quality report says supply comes from Big Elk Creek, four deep wells in the Potomac aquifer and an interconnection with Artesian Water Maryland, with the plant and wells operated under contract by Inframark. With several sources blending, taste and scale may differ by address.

The Town's 2025 water quality report does not list a total hardness figure. So test your tap, and ask the Town for its latest report before you buy a softener. Scale and sediment collect in a tank water heater over the years, which matters when you plan a water heater replacement.

Big Elk Creek, flooding and the low streets

The Town's own planning documents say Elkton has a long history of flooding along Big Elk Creek, with Delaware Avenue (MD 7), Howard Street and Bridge Street (MD 213) regularly submerged. FEMA maps roughly 16 percent of the town area in a 1 percent annual chance flood zone, and tidal surges from the Elk River can add to creek flooding in heavy storms.

If your home is on low ground near the creek, a basement sump and a backwater valve on the sewer line are worth asking about. Those same roads can close in a flood, so give the nearest cross street when you call.

Old downtown, newer subdivisions

The village at Head of Elk dates back to 1666, and the Town now has a Historic District with a Historic District Commission that reviews changes to structures there. If a plumbing job would alter the outside of a historic building, such as a new vent through a roof, ask the Town whether the Commission needs to see it. Houses that old often carry old supply lines and drains.

Newer growth is mostly in the northeast, north of US 40, in areas such as Patriots Glen, Washington Woods and Grays Hill. Elkton added almost 1,900 housing units between 1990 and 2005. An original tank water heater in those homes is now far past typical service life if it is still the original, so a leak at its base usually means replacement, not a patch.
