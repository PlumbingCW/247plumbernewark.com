Sewage backing up: what to do in the first few minutes

  1. Stop all water useEvery flush, shower, dishwasher cycle and load of laundry sends more water toward a line that has nowhere to put it.
  2. Keep people out of the areaSewage can carry bacteria, so children and pets should stay out of the wet room.
  3. Do not run the dishwasher or washing machineBoth discharge a lot of water at once and push the backup into the lowest drain in the house.
  4. Look at the other fixturesNote whether the basement floor drain, the toilets and the tub are all affected. That tells the plumber the blockage is in the main line or lateral.
  5. Protect what you canLift rugs and cardboard off the floor, and keep electrical devices out of standing water.
  6. Call us and tell the dispatcher everything you sawA licensed plumber can then arrive with the right cable and a plan for the cleanout.

Who clears which pipe in Newark

The City of Newark takes care of more than 100 miles of sanitary sewer main under the streets. The pipe from your house to that main is called the lateral. According to the City's Sewer Care Guide, a blockage in the lateral is for the owner to arrange to have cleared, while the City clears blockages in its own main.

The City's Water Resources team is available around the clock for sewer backups. Its crew generally checks the main under the street first, and if the main is clear, the next call is to a plumber for your lateral.

The municipal code (Sec. 25-2) adds a wrinkle for roots. A property-line cleanout changes who handles root clearing between the line and the main, so ask the plumber to locate yours before assuming the whole lateral is on your tab. How a sewer lateral gets cleaned

The plumber starts at the cleanout. If your home has one outside near the foundation, or at the property line, the cable goes in there. If it does not, access may come through a basement cleanout, a roof vent, or a pulled toilet. A drum machine feeds a heavy cable with a cutting head through the line until it reaches the blockage.

What the cable meets tells the story. Soft resistance with grease on the cable points to buildup. A sudden stop followed by grinding and then root fibers on the head points to roots. A hard stop that does not give can mean a collapsed pipe or an offset joint, and that is when a camera belongs in the conversation.

For heavy grease or repeat trouble, a high-pressure jet cleans the pipe wall more fully than a cable does. A cable restores flow, and a jet restores capacity.

Cleaning a sewer line before it backs up

Not every cleaning is an emergency. Some owners have a lateral cleaned after a close call, before a rental turnover, or after buying a home with no sewer history. A planned cleaning is less disruptive than a Saturday-night backup, and it lets the plumber see what the line looks like.

There is no set cleaning schedule. Roots, grease habits, pipe material and slope all matter. A better rule is to clean when symptoms appear, run a camera when they repeat, and repair when the camera shows damage.

Backflow prevention and insurance

The City's guide suggests considering a backflow prevention device that stops sewage from coming back into the house. A device does not clear a clog. It stops backed-up water from reaching your lowest fixtures, and a plumber can tell you whether your basement layout allows one.

The same guide notes that many homeowner policies exclude sewage backup. Ask your agent whether sewer backup coverage is available, and do it before an incident, because adding coverage after a loss is too late.
