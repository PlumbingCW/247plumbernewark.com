What to do before the plumber arrives

Find the main shutoff

Turn the home's main water valve clockwise until it stops. Do this first if water is spraying or a pipe has split.

Kill power where water is standing

Switch off the breaker for any outlet, appliance or panel section near water. Do not wade into a flooded basement to reach the panel.

Do not use drains

If a toilet or floor drain is backing up, stop running water, laundry and dishwashers. Every gallon you send down has nowhere to go.

Smell gas? Get out

Exit the building before anything else. Call the gas utility and 911 from the street, then a plumber. Gas piping is licensed work.

Photograph the water and the damage before you move anything. Homeowners policies often draw a line between sudden pipe failure and flood or sewage backup, so read yours before you file.

Homes built before 1980, and what that means for the pipes

About 77 percent of Brookside homes were built before 1980, according to the Census Bureau's 2020-2024 estimates, and the largest group, about 35 percent, dates to the 1950s. A house from that era can still have its original cast iron drain stack, galvanized steel supply lines or a patched mix of copper and newer plastic. We cannot tell which you have without looking, and the Census figures cover the whole area, not any single street.

Older drains are where storm backups show up first. Roots, scale inside iron pipe and a settled sewer line narrow the path, so heavy rain exposes a marginal drain. A sewer camera inspection shows the actual condition of the line before you decide between cleaning, spot repair and replacement.
