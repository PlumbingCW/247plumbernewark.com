How cold Newark gets, and why it matters

The 1991-2020 climate normals for the University of Delaware's Newark Ag Farm station put the January average minimum at 25.1 degrees Fahrenheit. Individual nights fall well below that average, and a house without heat cools fast. Pipes in exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces freeze first.

The same station records about 47 inches of precipitation a year. In a basement that takes on water, controls and burners near the floor do not survive submersion, so never run a boiler that has stood in floodwater until a licensed professional inspects it.

No heat right now: what you can check safely

  • Confirm the thermostat is set to heat, set above room temperature, and has fresh batteries if it uses them.
  • Check that the boiler's power switch and the breaker are on, and that any red emergency switch near the stairs has not been flipped off.
  • Read the display or note any blinking light pattern, and photograph it for the plumber.
  • Look at the pressure gauge. A reading near zero means the system has lost water, and a very high reading means the expansion tank or relief valve has a problem.
  • For an oil boiler, check the tank gauge. For gas, check that other gas appliances work, which tells you the supply is on.
  • Try the reset button once, according to the label. On an oil boiler, press it once only, then call. Repeated resets can flood a burner chamber or hide a real fault.

Stop there. Opening the burner compartment, touching the gas valve or removing the vent invites fire, gas leaks and carbon monoxide.

Hydronic basics: the parts that fail

A hydronic boiler heats water, a pump moves it through pipes, and radiators, baseboard or in-floor tubing release the heat. Not every house has one, so confirm what you have before calling. These are the parts that usually matter.

Low water pressure

A system that has lost water shuts down on a low-water cutoff or runs poorly. The cause can be a leak, a stuck relief valve or a failing auto-fill valve. Topping off without finding the leak only delays the problem.

Trapped air

Gurgling or a radiator that is cold on top points to air. Bleeding a radiator with a key is a common homeowner task, but do not open the boiler's relief valve, which is a safety device.

Circulator pump

If the boiler fires but the radiators stay cold, the pump may have failed or seized. A pump that is hot to the touch with no flow is a classic sign.

Expansion tank

A waterlogged tank cannot absorb heat expansion, so the relief valve may drip and the pressure swings high.

Flue and venting

A blocked or corroded flue can trip safety switches and push exhaust gases into the house.

Carbon monoxide and yearly service

The State Fire Marshal's winter guidance recommends a professional cleaning and inspection of chimneys and vents every year. It also advises carbon monoxide alarms outside each sleeping area and on every level, tested monthly.

Delaware's carbon monoxide statute, as we read it, covers lodging and rental establishments, so do not assume a law decides this for you. The Fire Marshal's advice still suits any home with a fuel-burning boiler or water heater. If an alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911.

If your hot water also stopped, the boiler may feed it through a separate tank, or you may have a standalone unit. The water heater repair page covers the second case.
