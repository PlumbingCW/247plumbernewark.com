Commercial plumbing differs from house calls because more people share the pipes, and the building owner, the tenant and the property manager may each have a say. This page covers the urgent cases and the recurring maintenance that heads them off.

Business drain backing up after hours

Commercial drains carry more load than a home. Kitchen waste, mop water, restroom traffic and floor sinks all feed lines that were sized for an earlier use. When one backs up, the first move is to stop adding water, which means pausing dishwashers, handwashing and restroom use on that branch.

Check whether one fixture or several are affected. A single slow sink points to a local clog, but several fixtures gurgling or overflowing together mean the main building drain is restricted. A plumber can then clear it with a cable or hydro-jetting equipment and run a camera to see why it blocked.

Repeat blockages in commercial lines usually have a cause that cable work alone does not fix, such as a belly in the pipe, roots in an old lateral or grease buildup. For the building-to-street portion of the line, see sewer line cleaning.

  4. Call the owner or managerA landlord may control the main line or hold the maintenance contract.

Backflow testing

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination. The Delaware plumbing regulation, 16 DE Admin Code 4455, states that owners of backflow assemblies must test them at least annually, as an amendment to the IPC. That duty falls on the owner, so a tenant should ask the landlord who holds the test records.

A licensed plumber can test the assembly, repair or replace worn relief valves and check valves, and file the report. Missed tests are easy to overlook because the assembly sits in a mechanical room or ceiling. Newark has a cross connection control program under the state backflow rules, so ask the City which reports your building must file.

Grease trap cleaning

Restaurants, cafeterias and food-prep kitchens often have a grease trap or interceptor that catches fats before they reach the sewer. A full trap lets grease pass into the lateral, where it cools and hardens. The City of Newark's sewer guide advises against putting fats, oils and grease down drains, and the owner is responsible for stoppages the building causes in its lateral.

Pump-out frequency depends on how much you cook and how large the trap is. A plumber can also look for broken baffles and failing lids. Whether your business needs a specific program is a question for the City, because Newark did not adopt New Castle County's commercial FOG section into its own code.

Storm drain cleaning

Parking-lot inlets, roof leaders and area drains fill with leaves, silt and trash, so rain pools at doorways. The City says much of the flooding in Newark comes from storm system capacity limits during heavy rain. A clean private drain cannot fix that, but it keeps your own site from being the weak link.

Newark's code bars connecting stormwater, sump pump discharge and subsoil drains to the sanitary sewer. A plumber will check where a drain actually goes, and a camera can show whether an old connection was ever tied in the wrong place.

Leak detection for commercial buildings

Finding a hidden leak is useful, but results vary by building and pipe layout, so we keep expectations plain. A plumber can isolate zones, pressure test sections and use sensing tools to narrow where water is going. Large buildings with slab runs or shared walls may need more than one visit. See leak detection for how that process works.

Older buildings and Main Street

The City's plan describes older Newark areas as built with a more traditional, denser design. In an older mixed-use building, drain lines may be shared between units or run in ways no drawing shows. A camera inspection before cutting into a floor or wall saves guesswork and helps identify which unit's pipe is at fault.

Property managers and landlords

Responsibility in a multi-tenant building depends on the lease. Common-area lines and the main drain are usually the landlord's, while fixtures inside a unit may be the tenant's. Document who approved the call.

A plumber needs access to ceilings, mechanical rooms and cleanouts, so line up keys and a contact before arrival. Keep written records of tests, pump-outs and repairs, because insurers and the City may ask for them.

Delaware law requires a licensed plumber for plumbing services on rental property or any part offered for rent, and Newark requires a contractor license for paid plumbing work. Check licenses at delpros.delaware.gov. Work for businesses in Brookside and other areas outside the city often falls under county permitting, and the Brookside page lists those towns.
