Garbage Disposal Installation in Newark, DE: What the Job Involves and Who Can Do It

A like-for-like swap is a manageable afternoon, but a first-time install brings wiring, drain and permit questions into play.

Written by Newark 24/7 Plumbing · 2026

Garbage disposal installation in Newark, DE is a job a handy homeowner can do on a like-for-like swap, but a first-time install is a plumbing and electrical project, not a quick bolt-on. The sink flange, the drain trap, the dishwasher hose and the power supply all have to line up, and any one can leave you with a leak or a dead unit.

Under state law an owner may plumb their own home if it is not rented or for sale and a permit application is filed. That carve-out leaves out gas piping, and Newark's code treats new wiring separately. If the power supply or the drain layout needs changes, a licensed plumber and electrician are the right people.

If you already have a unit that hums, jams or leaks, read about garbage disposal repair first, because a repair is often cheaper than replacing it.

What installing a disposal really involves

Most of the work happens under the sink, and most of the problems come from planning, not tightening bolts. Walk through the list before you buy a unit.

  1. Check the cabinet and the drainMeasure the space under the sink. Look at the trap, the tailpiece and where the dishwasher drain connects.
  2. Confirm powerA disposal needs a switched outlet or a hardwired connection. If neither exists under the sink, a new circuit or receptacle is wiring work.
  3. Remove the old strainer or unitTurn off the breaker for the disposal circuit and confirm the unit is dead, or unplug it. Shut off the water supply to the sink, disconnect the trap and undo the mounting ring or flange nut.
  4. Mount the sink flangeSet a bead of plumber's putty under the flange, seat it in the sink opening and tighten the mounting assembly evenly.
  5. Attach the disposal and connect the drainHang the unit on the mounting ring, rotate it into position, then connect the discharge pipe and trap. Knock out the dishwasher port plug only if a dishwasher will connect.
  6. Connect power and testOnce every joint is tight, restore power, run water, check for drips, then run the unit with water on.

The knockout plug is a classic mistake. If you connect a dishwasher hose without removing it, the dishwasher will not drain. If you remove it with no dishwasher attached, water pours out of the open port.

Disposal types and what changes

Continuous feed

Switch-operated and the usual choice for home kitchens. Easy to retrofit when a switch and outlet already exist.

Batch feed

Runs only when a cover is twisted into place. Often chosen for safety, but fewer models are sold.

Cord-connected versus hardwired

A cord plugged into a switched outlet is simpler. Hardwiring needs the electrical box and connection to be done correctly.

Horsepower and mount style

Larger motors run quieter with less jamming, and mount style must match the flange. Brand-to-brand swaps often reuse the same mounting ring.

DIY, plumber or both

A like-for-like swap on an existing circuit with the same drain layout is the easiest case. The harder cases are the ones where something is new or has to move.

If you have a leak after installing, do not keep running the unit. Most leaks come from the flange putty, the mounting ring or the discharge gasket, and a slow drip behind the cabinet can ruin wood within weeks.

Permits, wiring and Newark's rules

Local note, Newark and New Castle County

Inside the City of Newark, the code requires separate permits for plumbing and electrical work. Homeowners working on their own residence get an exception, as long as no part of the building is rented. The same pattern holds for the contractor license: Newark's license rule does not apply to an owner working on their own non-rental home.

The wiring line is where first-time installers trip up. Newark's electrical exemption covers minor repair work, and the code says that excludes installing any new wiring. That points to a permit for a brand-new outlet or circuit for a disposal. The City's permit form also has a field for an electrical inspection agency, and warns that review runs one to two weeks.

The code does not say whether swapping a disposal counts as replacing a fixture in kind. The code allows like-for-like fixture swaps without a plumbing permit as long as no pipes or valves move, but we did not find a statement that places a disposal in that category. Put the question to Code Enforcement before you start. If your parcel number begins with 18, the City issues the permit. If not, ask New Castle County Land Use.

For a quick answer, call Code Enforcement with your parcel number and describe the swap. Ask whether a plumbing permit is needed and whether a new circuit would require an electrical inspection. Write down the answer and who gave it.

State law adds a second layer. The state side has its own gate. Its homeowner exception, 24 Del. C. § 1807, covers plumbing except gas piping, and only with an application for a permit. The state plumbing regulation narrows it again to a single-family house the owner lives in, or will live in, for at least a year. Rentals and homes being sold do not qualify.

Sewer, septic and what the disposal sends down the drain

Newark's sewer code defines "shredded garbage" as particles no larger than half an inch, and the New Castle County code bans larger solids in the public sewer for unincorporated areas. That county rule is not adopted inside city limits, so do not treat it as a Newark ban. The practical point stays the same: a disposal is for soft scraps, not bones, peels in bulk or fibrous stalks.

Grease is the real enemy. Newark's sewer guide blames fats, oils and grease for blockages and backups. Its code also puts a grease or soap stoppage in the lateral on the owner. Running a disposal does not make grease safe to pour down the sink. If a disposal feeds a line that clogs often, drain cleaning may be the next step.

If your home is on septic instead of the public sewer, the EPA says a disposal means pumping the tank more often. If you live in a nearby town such as Glasgow or Bear, confirm whether your house is on public sewer or septic before you add a disposal.

What a plumber adds

A plumber installing a disposal handles the pieces that fail quietly. That includes the drain slope, a secure dishwasher connection, a correct trap arrangement and a leak test with water running. For jobs that tie into other work, such as a new sink or a rerouted line, the work often falls under plumbing installation and the plumber can check the whole sink drain at once.

Cost depends on whether wiring is needed, how complicated the drain is, the unit you choose and whether old plumbing has to be replaced. The plumber who comes out gives a clear price before work starts, and a same-day appointment may be available if your sink is out of service.
