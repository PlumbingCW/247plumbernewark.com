How Often Should You Clean a Sewer Line in Newark, DE?

There is no single interval that suits every house. Here is how roots, age and pipe type decide when a Newark, DE sewer line needs work.

Written by Newark 24/7 Plumbing · 2026

Open 24/7

Call now

There is no set schedule for cleaning a sewer line, and anyone who gives you one number for every house is guessing. In Newark, DE the better answer is to clean the line when signs, conditions or a camera look say it needs it, and to have it checked sooner if your house has the risk factors below.

A line with no roots, no sags and sound material can go years without work. A line under a mature tree, or one that has backed up before, may need attention far more often.

What the sewer line is, and where your part ends

The sewer line, or lateral, is the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the City's main under the street. The main belongs to the City. The lateral is split between you and the City by a rule in the Newark Code, which matters when you decide who calls whom.

For a pipe that has failed or a blockage that has already happened, see sewer line repair. This page is about deciding when to clean before trouble starts.

Signs the line needs attention now

  • Drains in more than one room run slowly at the same time
  • Gurgling in a toilet or tub when another fixture drains
  • Water backing up into a basement floor drain or a shower
  • A sewer odor outdoors near the cleanout, or in the lowest room
  • Soggy or unusually green patches of lawn along the line
  • A backup that cleared and then returned within a few months

One slow sink is a local clog. Several symptoms at once, or any backup into the house, point to the main line or the lateral. Stop using water and call the same day when sewage comes up.

Material and age change the picture. Clay pipe with many short joints is easy for roots to enter. Cast iron can scale and roughen inside. Plastic pipe has fewer joints and resists roots, but a poor slope can still hold waste. You cannot read any of this from the street, so a camera look decides.

Roots: the usual reason for repeat cleaning

Tree roots find the tiny amount of moisture and nutrients at a pipe joint. They enter as hair-like threads and grow into a mat that catches paper and grease. A cleaning cable or a water jet cuts the roots back, but they regrow, and how fast depends on the tree and the opening.

So a pipe with roots is not "cleaned" for good. It is on a cycle, and the cycle length comes from what the plumber sees on camera after a cleaning. If the roots return in a year, the visit schedule follows that. If the pipe stays clear for years, you stop paying for visits you do not need.

A cracked joint may need repair to end the cycle, and hydro jetting can clear scale and grease that a cable leaves behind.

Who clears what in Newark, DE

Local note, Newark and New Castle County

Section 25-2 of the Newark Code divides the lateral at the property line, and the dividing mark is a cleanout. The City handles roots only in the stretch between the property line and the main, and only where a cleanout sits at or near that line. Where no cleanout exists, the owner is the one who clears the roots.

The owner also answers for stoppages from grease and soap that leave the house, and for a damaged pipe between the property line and the building. A failed lateral on the street side of that line is the City's to repair or replace. If roots can only come out by digging in the street right-of-way, the City digs and repairs there and nowhere else.

Installing a cleanout at the property line is how an owner asks the City to take over right-of-way roots. New sewers and replacement sewers must have one. For you, that cleanout is the most useful feature on the line, because it gives a camera access and settles who handles roots.

Not every address is inside the City. Homes in unincorporated areas around Newark, such as parts of Glasgow and Bear, sit under New Castle County rules, and some are on septic systems instead of sewer.

If you are on a septic tank, the clock is different. Septic tanks run on their own cycle, which the EPA puts at pumping every few years, sooner if a disposal is in use. That is a tank, not a sewer line, and your plumber or a septic contractor will tell you what your system needs.

How to decide: a short process

  1. Find your cleanoutLook for a capped pipe in the yard or against the foundation. A missing cleanout is itself a reason to talk to a plumber.
  2. Count your risk factorsLarge trees near the line, an older house and any past backup each raise the odds.
  3. Ask for a camera lookA camera shows roots, cracks, sags and scale. The footage tells you whether to clean, repair or leave it alone.
  4. Set the next date from what the camera showsClear pipe and few roots means a long gap. Fresh roots or heavy buildup means a shorter one.
  5. Keep the recordSave the video and notes. They help with the next visit and a home sale.

A sewer camera inspection turns a vague guess into a date. Treat the camera as the decision tool, not the cleaning.

What goes down the drain matters as much as the calendar

Newark's sewer guidance names fats, oils, grease, rags and diapers as blockage makers. The EPA says only toilet paper belongs in the toilet and lists wipes among the causes of sewer blockages. These habits affect how often you need a plumber more than any schedule.

Grease also decides who pays. A stoppage caused by what you pour down the kitchen drain is the owner's bill under city code.

Insurance and backups

A City crew will check the main under the street if your house backs up, and the City says it is available 24/7 through its Water Resources Department. If the main is clear, the lateral is your job. The City also says it usually cannot cover cleanup or damage costs from a backup.

Many policies do not cover water or sewage backing up into a home, and some offer it as an added rider. Ask your agent. The City also suggests considering a device that stops water from backing up into the house.

When to call

Call a licensed plumber after any backup, or when two or more fixtures run slow. You can verify the plumber through the state's DELPROS license lookup. For a line that needs clearing now, sewer line cleaning is the service that restores flow.
